# TypeSafe (Jev) on a real database — reference resolution, 2026-09-18

Third follow-up to [the executor probe](typesafe-executor-probe-2026-09.md),
which resolved mentions against synthetic lists of 2–4 records. Here the
records live in Postgres and candidates come from the modules' own search.
Harness: `apps/api/src/typesafe-probe/base/` — a db test that always seeds
and checks the world, and calls Jev only with `TYPESAFE_LIVE_PROBE=1`
(owner-authorised hand-runs; three runs, about $0.03).

## Setup

A throwaway Testcontainers database (the db-test harness), company A and
staff member Anna from the core test kit. Seeded **through the real create
actions**: 300 customers (people with shared first names and near surnames,
ТОВ/ФОП, Latin-script names), 134 products (drinks × variants, bakery, coffee
beans by origin and pack), 12 customer groups. 42 labelled mentions:
inflected, surname-only, diminutive, misspelled, transliterated, branch of a
company, ambiguous on purpose, and absent on purpose.

Three ways to resolve a mention:

- **Retrieval → Jev**: `customers.listCustomers` / `catalog.listProducts` /
  `customers.listGroups` with the mention as the search, limit 50; Jev picks
  among what came back (`ambiguous` and `none` always offered); zero
  candidates means `none` without a call.
- **Whole list → Jev**: every record name of that kind as the options, no
  search. Above 150 names the list is asked in chunks and the chunk winners
  go to a final pick.
- **Combined**: retrieval first; the whole list only when retrieval returns
  nothing.

## Findings

- **When the module's search finds the record, Jev never picked wrong.**
  Every one of those mentions resolved correctly, including "ambiguous" on
  9, 12 and 20 real candidates (Коваленко, Олена, кава). Inflected full
  names are found by the existing stem search and resolved at 0.83–0.99.
- **The bottleneck is retrieval recall, not Jev.** The search found the
  record for 64% of mentions. It returns nothing for a typo ("капучіно",
  "питренко"), transliteration ("flat white" → Флет вайт, "анна ковальська"
  → Anna Kowalska), a diminutive ("саша", "олі"), extra words ("кава хаус на
  подолі", "кіло ефіопії в зернах"), and — for **groups** — any inflection
  at all ("оптовиків", "в ресторанах", "віп"): `customers.listGroups` search
  has no stemming, unlike customers and products.
- **Jev can replace retrieval on small lists.** Over all 134 product names
  and all 12 groups in one question it was **100% correct**, typos,
  Latin-for-Cyrillic and "кіло ефіопії в зернах" included, at about 3–5k
  tokens ($0.0002) a request.
- **One Choice takes at most 256 options** (HTTP 400 above; 250 names +
  `none` worked at 0.88 confidence). Not documented by TypeSafe; measured.
- **On 300 similar person names the whole list over-answers `ambiguous`**
  (55% correct alone): with a dozen Олена and several Петренко in view, a
  correct full name drops to 0.45–0.59 and `ambiguous` wins. It still
  rescues what search cannot find ("саша білий" → Олександр Білий at 0.91,
  "кофі поінт львів" → Coffee Point Lviv).
- **Combined: 90% correct (38/42); products and groups 100%, customers
  82%.** Of the four misses three are `ambiguous` — a picker, not an error.
- **The one wrong record is the class to fear:** "олена петрашенко" — a
  customer who does not exist — came back as Олена Петренко at **0.91** from
  the whole-list fallback. The same mention answered `ambiguous` (0.30) in
  the previous run with identical input, so answers near a decision boundary
  are **not stable run to run**. A confirmation card naming the customer is
  what stands between this and a wrong order; confidence alone does not.

## Reading

- Reference resolution on a real base is viable with today's search for
  exact and inflected names, and Jev adds real value exactly where search is
  blind — but a whole-list fallback for *people* must never auto-accept: a
  near-miss surname is matched confidently. Products and groups are safe to
  resolve over the whole list while a company has fewer than ~250 of them.
- Two module gaps surfaced that are independent of Jev: group search has no
  stemming, and neither customer nor product search tolerates a typo or
  transliteration (catalog trigram similarity did not fire for "капучіно").
  Both hurt the classic UI search and the Sonnet assistant today.
- Evidence limits: one seeded company, names generated by the proposing
  agent, 42 mentions, one model version, and visible run-to-run variance.

## After the one-name-matcher change (same day)

The module gaps above were fixed on the same branch (`docs/specs/db.md`,
"One staff name matcher"): every list, resolver and `searchMatches` now
shares one matcher in `@showzy/module-kit/name-match` — word start on the
stem (inflections) everywhere, and a substring / typo fallback when that
finds nothing. The probe was re-run unchanged.

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval finds the record | 64% | **73%** (products 67% → 83%) |
| Correct via retrieval → Jev | 69% | **76%** |
| Correct combined | 90% | **93%** |
| Mean candidates per mention | 2.1 | 2.5 |

- Typos now resolve through retrieval: "олена питренко" → Олена Петренко
  (0.92), "капучіно" → Капучино (0.96); group "оптовиків" → Оптовики (0.94).
- A first version of the typo tier used the bare trigram operator and
  returned 34 candidates for "олена питренко" — Ukrainian surnames share
  «-енко». Requiring `word_similarity >= 0.5` on top of the index operator
  brought that to 3 with the same recall.
- What retrieval still cannot find is what was declared out of scope:
  transliteration ("flat white", "анна ковальська", "віп"), diminutives
  ("саша", "олі") and mentions carrying extra words ("в ресторанах", "кава
  хаус на подолі", "кіло ефіопії в зернах" — every token must match). The
  whole-list fallback still rescues most of them.
- The confident wrong record is unchanged and now has a second source: the
  typo tier itself offers Олена Петренко for the non-existent "олена
  петрашенко" (Jev: 0.83). For a human picker that is the right "did you
  mean"; for a model it means **a candidate that came from the typo tier
  must never be auto-accepted** — the resolvers already enforce exactly that
  rule (`isInflectionOfName` or exact, otherwise a picker).
